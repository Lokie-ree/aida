# Teacher Pain Points Research - Louisiana Educators

**Research Date:** November 3, 2025  
**Method:** Firecrawl MCP search and web scraping  
**Sources:** Louisiana Department of Education, Louisiana teacher associations, education policy documents

---

## Executive Summary

Research identifies critical pain points affecting Louisiana educators' daily work. These findings inform framework development priorities and demonstrate Pelican AI's alignment with real educator needs.

---

## Key Pain Points Identified

### 1. Lesson Planning Burdens

**Severity:** High  
**Frequency:** Daily/Weekly

**Findings:**
- Teachers forced to write lesson plans that are "burdensome and sometimes do not align with curricula"
- Causes "unnecessary paperwork and many hours of additional work, often outside of school hours"
- Antiquated lesson planning requirements/templates mandated at school/system level
- Lesson plans don't always match high-quality curricula that already provide plans

**Quotes from Sources:**
> "Some teachers are forced to write lesson plans that are burdensome and sometimes do not align with curricula. This causes unnecessary paperwork and many hours..."

> "Effective teachers should have professional autonomy in their classrooms. Teachers with an evaluation rating of proficient or higher should be allowed to internalize lessons within their curriculum and plan to use those materials to meet the individualized needs of their students."

**Framework Implications:**
- Need frameworks that help internalize lessons quickly
- Support for unpacking standards and creating objectives efficiently
- Templates that work with existing curricula, not replace them

---

### 2. Planning Time Constraints

**Severity:** Critical  
**Frequency:** Daily

**Findings:**
- Louisiana law mandates 45 minutes of uninterrupted planning time (Act 392) but it's often pulled for other duties
- Planning time used to cover classes due to teacher shortages
- Teachers need planning time to:
  - Assess student work
  - Review relevant curriculum
  - Prepare lessons
  - Draft lesson plans
  - Make copies
  - Find resources for students

**Quote from Source:**
> "Teachers need adequate planning time to assess student work, review relevant curriculum, prepare their lesson, draft lesson plans, make copies, find resources for their students to use, and more."

> "For many teachers, their planning time is the only point in the day where they have time to drink some water, eat, or use the restroom."

**Framework Implications:**
- Focus on frameworks that save significant time (validates our 3-5 hours/week goal)
- Multi-step frameworks that accomplish multiple tasks efficiently
- Frameworks that work within planning period constraints

---

### 3. Standards Unpacking and Alignment

**Severity:** High  
**Frequency:** Weekly/Unit Planning

**Findings:**
- Need to unpack Louisiana state standards efficiently
- Create differentiated "I can" statements aligned to standards
- Difficulty finding and accessing LDOE resources and guidance
- Need for clear alignment with Louisiana Educator Rubric (LER)

**Framework Implications:**
- **Critical need:** Standards unpacking framework (already have IEH-001)
- Louisiana-specific standards alignment is high value
- LER domain alignment is essential for teacher evaluation

---

### 4. Differentiation Challenges

**Severity:** High  
**Frequency:** Daily

**Findings:**
- Challenges in supporting diverse learners
- Need for differentiated assessment practices
- Creating student-friendly directions and supports
- Meeting needs of English learners and students with disabilities

**Framework Implications:**
- Differentiation frameworks are high priority
- Multi-level scaffolded approaches needed
- Assessment differentiation frameworks valuable

---

### 5. Communication and Administrative Tasks

**Severity:** Medium  
**Frequency:** Weekly

**Findings:**
- Parent communication takes significant time
- Newsletters and updates time-consuming
- Professional email drafting challenges

**Framework Implications:**
- Communication frameworks already exist (AIB-001, AIB-002) - validated need
- Time-saving focus is critical

## Louisiana Educator Rubric (LER) - Official Framework

**Source:** Louisiana Department of Education (Released April 2024)  
**Partnership:** LDOE and National Institute for Excellence in Teaching (NIET)  
**URL:** https://doe.louisiana.gov/docs/default-source/key-compass-resources/louisiana-educator-rubric.pdf

### Overview

The Louisiana Educator Rubric was designed through a partnership between the Louisiana Department of Education and NIET to directly support improvements in classroom instruction. The rubric provides educators with a common understanding and language for designing and planning instruction and using effective instructional practices to support student learning.

**Key Features:**
- Grounded in NIET's Teaching and Learning Standards Rubric (20 years of research, 21 states)
- Used in environments ranging from urban to rural areas
- Applicable to all subjects and modalities
- Performance levels: 5 (Exemplary), 3 (Proficient), 1 (Unsatisfactory), with 2 and 4 available based on evidence

### LER Four Domains and Indicators

#### Domain 1: INSTRUCTION (12 Indicators)
1. **Standards and Objectives (SO)**
   - Exemplary: All learning objectives and state content standards explicitly communicated and understood by students; students make connections between objectives and prior learning/life experiences
   - Critical alignment: Objectives aligned to depth and rigor of state standards; lesson content aligned to high-quality instructional materials

2. **Motivating Students (MS)**
   - Content organized to be personally meaningful, relevant, and intellectually engaging
   - Inquiry, curiosity, and exploration valued

3. **Presenting Instructional Content (PIC)**
   - Visuals, examples, modeling, criteria for success
   - Logical sequencing and segmenting

4. **Lesson Structure and Pacing (LS)**
   - Coherent structure based on content, organized to meet students' needs
   - Brisk pacing adjusted for rigor and individual student learning expectations

5. **Activities and Materials (ACT)**
   - Support lesson objectives; challenging; elicit variety of thinking
   - Student-centered: sustain attention, provide student-to-student interaction, evoke curiosity

6. **Questioning (QU)**
   - High-quality questions with appropriate mix: knowledge/comprehension, application/analysis, creation/evaluation
   - Wait time (3-5 seconds) consistently provided
   - Students generate questions leading to further inquiry

7. **Academic Feedback (FEED)**
   - Academically focused, frequent, high-quality feedback
   - Teacher circulates to prompt thinking and assess progress
   - Students give specific feedback to each other

8. **Grouping Students (GRP)**
   - Grouping arrangements maximize understanding and learning efficiency
   - Each student takes responsibility for individual role and group work expectations
   - Students set goals, reflect on, and evaluate learning in groups

9. **Teacher Content Knowledge (TCK)**
   - Extensive content knowledge of state standards and high-quality instructional materials
   - Implements subject-specific instructional strategies
   - Highlights key concepts and connects powerful ideas

10. **Teacher Knowledge of Students (TKS)**
    - Understanding of each student's anticipated learning abilities and needs
    - Incorporates student interests and backgrounds
    - Provides differentiated supports to ensure students master grade-level standards

11. **Thinking (TH)**
    - Students actively engaged in multiple types of thinking:
      - Analytical: analyze, compare/contrast, evaluate, explain
      - Practical: use, apply, implement in real-life scenarios
      - Creative: create, design, imagine, suppose
      - Research-based: explore and review variety of ideas, models, solutions

12. **Problem-Solving (PS)**
    - Students engage in problem-solving types: categorization, drawing conclusions/justifying solutions, predicting outcomes, observing and experimenting, improving solutions, identifying relevant/irrelevant information, generating ideas, creating and designing

#### Domain 2: PLANNING (3 Indicators)

1. **Instructional Plans (IP)**
   - Evidence of internalization of plans from high-quality curriculum
   - Measurable and explicit objectives aligned to state standards (content and rigor)
   - Activities, materials, and assessments:
     - Aligned to state standards, content (including HQ curriculum), and success criteria
     - Sequenced and scaffolded based on student need
     - Build on prior student knowledge, relevant to students' lives
     - Provide appropriate time for student work, reflection, and lesson closure
   - Plan appropriate for age, knowledge, and interests of all learners
   - Regular opportunities to accommodate individual student needs
   - **Exemplary includes:** Strategies for student autonomy and ownership

2. **Student Work (SW)**
   - Assignments aligned to rigor and depth of standards and curriculum content
   - Aligned to lesson's objective with descriptions of how assessment results will inform future instruction
   - Students: organize, interpret, analyze, synthesize, and evaluate information rather than reproduce it
   - Draw conclusions, make generalizations, produce arguments supported through extended writing
   - Connect learning to experiences, observations, feelings, or situations significant in daily lives

3. **Assessment (AS)**
   - Aligned with depth and rigor of state standards and content (including curriculum resources)
   - Designed to provide feedback on progress against objectives
   - Use variety of question types and formats to gauge student learning and problem-solving
   - Measure student performance in more than three ways (project, experiment, presentation, essay, short answer, multiple choice)
   - Require extended written tasks as appropriate
   - Include clear illustrations of student progress toward state standards, which students monitor, understand, and articulate
   - Include descriptions of how assessment results will be used by teachers and students to inform future instruction and learning

#### Domain 3: ENVIRONMENT (4 Indicators)

1. **Expectations (ES)**
   - Clear and rigorous academic expectations
   - Actively uses aligned and differentiated high-quality materials and resources to ensure access to learning
   - Students regularly learn from mistakes and describe thinking on what they learned
   - Teacher creates learning opportunities where all students consistently experience success
   - Students lead opportunities that support learning
   - Students take initiative to meet or exceed teacher expectations

2. **Engaging Students and Managing Behavior (ESMB)**
   - Students consistently engaged in behaviors that optimize learning and increase time on task
   - Teacher and students establish commitments for learning and behavior (Exemplary)
   - Teacher uses variety of techniques (rewards, approval, contingent activities, consequences) that maintain student engagement and promote positive learning environment

3. **Environment (ENV)**
   - Classroom: welcomes all students and guests; provides safe space for all students to take risks and interact with peers
   - Clearly organized and designed for and with students to promote learning for all
   - Supplies, equipment, and resources easily and readily accessible to provide opportunities for all students
   - Displays current student work that promotes learning

4. **Respectful Conditions (RC)**
   - Teacher-student and student-student interactions consistently demonstrate caring, kindness, and respect for one another
   - Celebrate and acknowledge all students' backgrounds
   - Teacher seeks out and is receptive to interests and opinions of all students
   - Positive relationships and interdependence among students

#### Domain 4: PROFESSIONALISM (4 Indicators)

1. **Growing and Developing Professionally**
   - Prompt, prepared, and participates in professional learning and teacher collaboration meetings
   - Appropriately attempts to implement new learning in classroom
   - Develops and works on plan for new learning based on school improvement plans, self-assessment, and input from observations
   - Participates in reflection and growth by selecting specific activities, content knowledge, or pedagogical skills to enhance proficiency

2. **Reflecting on Teaching**
   - Makes thoughtful and accurate assessments of lessons' effectiveness
   - Offers specific actions to improve teaching
   - Accepts responsibilities contributing to school improvement
   - Utilizes student achievement data to address strengths and weaknesses of students and guide instructional decisions

3. **School Involvement**
   - Supports school activities and events

4. **School Responsibilities**
   - Accepts responsibility and/or assists peers in contributing to safe and orderly school environment
   - Adheres to system and school policies
   - Keeps timely and professional records

### Key Definitions from LER

**Expectations (also known as Success Criteria):** Refers to a concrete learning performance: something students will say, do, or make to indicate they are moving towards the learning goal/objective.

**High-quality instructional materials (HQIM):** Fully aligned to state content standards (or national/local when state standards not provided); builds content-specific knowledge and skills; provides support for all learners and high-quality resources for teachers; includes effective lesson structures and strategies, appropriate pacing guidance, meaningful connected assessments, and implementation guidance and resources.

## Louisiana Educator Advancement and Development System (LEADS)

**Source:** Louisiana Department of Education  
**URL:** https://doe.louisiana.gov/school-system-leaders/school-system-support/louisiana-educator-advancement-and-development-system-(leads)

### Overview

The Louisiana Department of Education is reimagining the state's educator evaluation system, guided by feedback from **over 8,000 Louisiana educators**. The initiative addresses key concerns within the education community, striving to develop a more responsive and impactful process that benefits both educators and students.

**Implementation Timeline:**
- **Statewide implementation:** Scheduled for 2025-2026 school year
- **Current status:** 2024-2025 is a "Learning Year" with evaluation options

### LEADS Key Objectives

- Use research-based teaching and leadership standards that provide clear descriptions of practice across a range of indicators
- Support professional growth and development through coaching and feedback
- Align with Louisiana Educator Rubric (LER) and Louisiana Leader Rubric
- Integrate with Louisiana Evaluation System (LES) - the technical platform

### LEADS Evaluation Components

1. **Louisiana Educator Rubric (LER)** - Primary evaluation tool for classroom teachers
2. **Louisiana Leader Rubric** - For school-level administrators
3. **Louisiana Counselor Rubric** - For school counselors
4. **Student Learning Targets (SLTs)** - Goal-setting and progress monitoring
5. **Professional Growth Plans (PGPs)** - Coaching and development plans
6. **Value-Added Model (VAM)** - For eligible courses (student growth measures)

## LER and LEADS Alignment with Pain Points

### Connection to Framework Development

**Pain Point #1: Lesson Planning Burdens**
- **LER Domain 2: Planning - Instructional Plans (IP)** requires:
  - Evidence of internalization of plans from high-quality curriculum
  - Objectives aligned to state standards (content and rigor)
  - Activities aligned to standards, content, and success criteria
  - **Framework Need:** Tools that help teachers internalize curriculum quickly and align plans to LER expectations

**Pain Point #2: Planning Time Constraints**
- **LER Domain 2: Planning** indicators require extensive planning work
- **Framework Need:** Multi-step frameworks that efficiently address multiple LER indicators simultaneously

**Pain Point #3: Standards Unpacking and Alignment**
- **LER Domain 1: Instruction - Standards and Objectives (SO)** requires:
  - All learning objectives and state content standards explicitly communicated
  - Objectives aligned to depth and rigor of state standards
  - **Framework Need:** Standards unpacking that directly produces LER-aligned objectives

**Pain Point #4: Differentiation Challenges**
- **LER Domain 1: Instruction - Teacher Knowledge of Students (TKS)** requires:
  - Understanding of each student's anticipated learning abilities and needs
  - Differentiated supports to ensure students master grade-level standards
- **LER Domain 2: Planning - Instructional Plans (IP)** requires:
  - Regular opportunities to accommodate individual student needs
  - Plan appropriate for age, knowledge, and interests of all learners
- **Framework Need:** Differentiation frameworks that produce LER-aligned plans and supports

**Pain Point #5: Communication and Administrative Tasks**
- **LER Domain 4: Professionalism** includes school involvement and responsibilities
- **Framework Need:** Communication tools that support professional responsibilities efficiently

### Critical Framework Alignment Requirements

1. **All frameworks must align with LER Domain indicators** to support teacher evaluation
2. **Standards-based work must reference Louisiana Student Standards** explicitly
3. **Planning frameworks must demonstrate internalization** of high-quality curriculum
4. **Differentiation must be explicit** in planning and instructional materials
5. **Assessment frameworks must align with LER Domain 2: Assessment** requirements (variety of question types, multiple measures, extended writing where appropriate)
